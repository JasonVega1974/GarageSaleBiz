One weekend, start to finish

What the job
actually looks like.

This is the sale on the demo site — the same one you can click into above. No inventory, no storefront, no van full of stock: the goods belong to the homeowner until they sell.

  1. Thu 6:10 pm

    You walk the garage with the homeowner, agree your rate, and both sign the agreement on your phone. Nothing gets moved until that is done.

  2. Fri 4:00 pm

    Price and stage. The good pieces get photographed onto your site so shoppers can see them before they decide to drive over.

  3. Sat 7:40 am

    Signs go up. Arrows on every corner within half a mile, and the big one at the mouth of the driveway.

  4. Sat 8:00 am

    Doors open. There are people in the driveway at 7:52. There always are.

  5. Sat 2:00 pm

    Everything drops 25%. The browsers from this morning come back for the thing they were thinking about all afternoon.

  6. Sun 11:00 am

    Half price, or make an offer. The goal on the last day is an empty garage, not a maximum price.

  7. Mon 9:00 am

    You settle up, hand the homeowner their share, and photograph the empty garage. That photo is how you get the next job.

You set your own rate. Operators in this trade commonly charge somewhere between 40% and 60% of what a sale takes in, depending on how much of the work is theirs — a full clear-out where you price, stage, staff, and clean up is worth more than a Saturday where the homeowner does half of it. The dashboard defaults to 50% and you change it per job. We do not set your prices and we make no promise about what you will earn.

Why I built this

I built EstateSaleBiz first. Estate sales are a solemn business — somebody has died, or is moving into care, and a family is standing in a full house making decisions they did not plan to make. Garage sales are the same job, smaller and lighter: the same walkthrough, the same pricing, the same signs on the corner, the same Monday morning with an empty driveway. The tools turned out to be very nearly the same tools, so I built them again for the smaller job.

There is a real job in that, and the people who would be good at it are usually not the people who want to build a website, write a contract, and work out what to charge.

So that is what this is: the parts that stop somebody starting, already built. The three cities are locked to one operator because a territory you have to share is not a territory.
